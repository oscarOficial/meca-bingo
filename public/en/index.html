How it works

One person hosts on a laptop or TV; everyone else plays from their phone.

Pick a playlist

Paste a link to a public playlist from Spotify, Deezer, Apple Music, YouTube Music or Amazon Music — or paste the songs.

Set the rules

Card type, what counts as a line, prizes, song length, language, your own photos and messages.

Friends scan the QR

Each phone gets a different card with its name. If someone reloads, they keep their card.

Play and call

Put the music on. Players tap what they hear and call LINE or BINGO; the host's screen says VALID or NOPE.

Make it yours

Every game can look and play differently.

🃏 Two card typesSpanish 3×9 with gaps (15 songs) or classic 5×5 (25 songs).
📏 Your rulesHorizontal lines only, or vertical too (and diagonals on 5×5).
🏆 Prizes in orderLine, two lines and full-card bingo — turn on the ones you want.
⏱ Song lengthWhole songs or 30 / 45 / 60 / 90 seconds for a faster game.
📸 Photo momentAfter each song one of your photos fills the screen with a funny caption.
💬 Your messages & languageEdit the captions for playing, valid and wrong calls. English and Spanish included.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. No accounts, no ads, no tracking.

Do players need an app or an account?

No. They scan the QR or type the 5-letter code in any phone browser and write their name.

Can two players get the same card?

No. Each phone gets a different card, and keeps it if it reloads.

How are calls checked?

Every marked song must have played, and the marks must complete the prize. Otherwise the host screen shows NOPE and the player sees which song was wrong.

Where is our data?

Everything stays in the host's browser; phones connect directly to it. Your photos never leave the host's computer.

Do I need the extension?

Only for Amazon Music playlists, or if you want songs to be marked automatically (playing from the Spotify, Amazon Music, Deezer, YouTube Music or Apple Music web player in the same computer). Without it, the host marks the song that is playing with two taps.

Do I need a license to play music in public?

Streaming accounts are for personal use. For bars or public events, check the music licensing rules of your country.
